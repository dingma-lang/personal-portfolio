01 / THE QUESTION

Can a policy story
be seen in mobility data?

The project investigates whether H-1B policy context was reflected in a different mobility pattern around selected university and technology-related areas. The testable question is whether those locations changed differently from other Boston–Cambridge points of interest between January 2025 and January 2026.

Graduate analytics coursework

BUS240F · Brandeis University · Spring 2026. The supplied presentation script describes Ding Ma’s work on the comparison design, cleaning, hypothesis test, diagnostics, and presentation.

02 / DATA & APPROACH

Make the comparison explicit.

The Advan dataset starts with 65,360 rows. Removing 113 coordinate outliers leaves 65,247 valid POI–week observations in Boston and Cambridge.

The focus group combines university POIs with the Kendall/MIT, Harvard, and BU/Fenway areas. Other local POIs form the comparison group. These locations are proxies for activity around universities and technology institutions, rather than direct measures of visa status.

Check locations→Compare groups→Test & contextualize
The model, in plain language

The model uses log(1 + visits), focus-group status, year, and their interaction. The interaction asks whether focus areas changed differently between the two January periods. The original report provides the specification and estimates.

03 / WHAT EMERGED

The groups moved together.

Focus areas and university POIs rose from 15,624,289 to 15,674,141 visits. Other local POIs rose from 42,469,410 to 42,602,968. At this aggregate level, the growth rates are almost identical.

THE TAKEAWAY

This dataset does not show clear evidence of a broad differential mobility disruption in the selected focus areas. Local variation still deserves attention.

04 / LIMITS & NEXT STEPS

A geographic proxy
has a boundary.

  • Mobility data does not identify visitors’ visa status, employment, or reason for visiting.
  • Only two January periods are observed. The comparison cannot establish pre-trends or isolate all policy effects.
  • Weather, transit, academic calendars, tourism, office attendance, and POI mix can affect the same outcome.
  • The external controls are city- or state-level proxies rather than individual or POI-level exposure measures.
  • The source diagnostics show noisy residuals and departures from normality. This is cautious comparative evidence rather than a strong causal design.

A stronger follow-up would extend the time window, examine pre-trends, connect locations to more direct exposure measures, and include relevant local controls.

The supplied presentation also sketches a January 2027 stability scenario. That is an assumption-based outlook, rather than a validated out-of-sample prediction, and is not presented here as a measured result.
